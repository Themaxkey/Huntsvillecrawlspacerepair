Nine times out of ten in a crawl space home, this is not your foundation. It is what is holding your floor up, and that is a much cheaper problem.

## The Difference That Saves You Thousands

**Foundation settlement** moves the perimeter of the house. It shows as cracking in exterior brick, doors out of square, and floors sloping consistently toward one outside wall. The fix is piers, and it runs into five figures.

**Interior support failure** moves the middle of the house. It shows as floors that dip or bounce toward the center of a room, often with no exterior cracking at all. The fix is jacks and footings under the house, and it is a fraction of the cost.

People frequently pay for the first when they needed the second. If a contractor quotes piers for a springy floor without going under the house, get another opinion.

## What Fails Underneath

**Piers settling.** A great many older Huntsville homes sit on dry-stacked block piers with no footing beneath them. Over decades they sink into soil that has been repeatedly wetted, and the floor follows.

**Girder or joist decay.** Where damp has kept wood above twenty percent moisture, decay softens the timber and it loses stiffness before it loses strength. Bounce is often the first sign.

**Undersized framing.** Some houses were built with joists spanning further than they comfortably should, and simply sag over time.

**Removed walls.** An interior wall taken out during a remodel was sometimes carrying load, and nothing was put back.

## How It Gets Fixed

Adjustable steel jacks on new poured footings, placed to shorten the span, and raised **gradually** — usually over weeks rather than in one visit. Lifting a settled floor quickly cracks plaster, drywall and tile upstairs.

Decayed girders and joists sistered with new lumber or replaced outright.

And then the moisture work, because supports that failed due to damp will fail again in a crawl space that stays wet.

## Cost

Support work typically runs from around $1,500 to $6,000 depending on how many points are needed and access. Joist and girder repair is generally in the low thousands. Compare that with piers at $1,500 to $3,500 each and the value of a correct diagnosis becomes obvious.

Free inspection, and somebody will actually go under the house.
