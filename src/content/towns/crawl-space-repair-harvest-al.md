Harvest has absorbed a great deal of Huntsville's growth, and like Madison its housing is mostly recent, built on ground that was farmland within living memory.

## What Recent Construction Brings

Level building pads on rolling ground mean cut and fill. Fill consolidates for years after the house is finished, and where a foundation bridges fill and undisturbed soil, the two sides move differently. That is the source of most structural cracking in the newer parts of Harvest, and it typically shows up in the first decade.

Crawl spaces here are usually shallow, which matters more than people realize. Eighteen inches of clearance makes every subsequent repair slower and therefore more expensive, and it makes it far more likely that nobody has been under there since the house was built.

## The Damp Is the Same as Everywhere Here

New or old, a vented crawl space in north Alabama takes in air at a low-seventies dew point through the summer and condenses it on framing that is cooler. The house being fifteen years old does not change the physics; it only means there is less accumulated damage to undo.

What we find in Harvest most often is elevated wood moisture with no visible rot yet, fiberglass insulation beginning to sag, and ductwork sweating. That is the ideal time to deal with it, because the fix is a liner, sealed vents and a dehumidifier rather than structural repair.

## Drainage Is Usually the First Job

Subdivision grading was signed off before the fences, patios and landscaping went in. On a lot where water now runs toward the house, the cheapest effective work is outside: extending downspouts well clear of the foundation and restoring fall away from the walls.

We check that before quoting anything interior. It would be easy not to, and the difference to you is thousands.
